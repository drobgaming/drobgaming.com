NOTES

From the Vault

Short reads. Tap one to open it — each takes under a minute.

Condition is everythingWhy one crushed corner can kill a dealRead →

In collectibles, the headline price is for the right copy — not any copy. Corners, seals and stickers get checked before a price does.

A crushed corner or a torn wrap doesn't make a piece “a little cheaper.” At the top end, it can take it out of the conversation entirely.

That's the house rule here: sealed means sealed, and flaws get told straight, up front.

Mega Man: 18 sealed copies, totalThe grail on the games shelfRead →

The sealed Mega Man for NES (1987) in the vault is graded WATA 9.4 with an A+ seal — Rev-A, Round SOQ, the “Dr. Wily” text variant, Captain Commando offer sticker still on the wrap.

WATA's population report counts just 18 sealed copies graded across all variants combined. That's the whole supply at that level — not 18 of this variant. Eighteen, period.

Libertads: mintage isn't the whole storyWhy survivors matter more than the number struckRead →

The 2004-Mo ½ oz gold Libertad had a mintage of just 500. Small to begin with.

But early Libertads were melted in big numbers, so the surviving count is likely far lower than 500. When you price one, you're pricing survivors — not the mintage on paper.

Longboxes: the packaging everybody threw awayWhy a sealed TMNT soundtrack is a findRead →

Early CDs came in tall cardboard longboxes. Almost everybody tore them open and threw them away on day one.

So a 1990 TMNT movie soundtrack still sealed in its longbox, bonus sticker intact, isn't just a CD. It's the packaging surviving — and that's the rare part.

Privacy Policy

Short version: this app collects nothing. Last updated November 18, 2026.

What this app collects

Nothing. The drobgaming app collects no personal data itself. There are no accounts, no sign-ups, no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking. Nothing you do in the app is recorded, stored, or sent anywhere by the app.

The want-list form

The want-list form in Shop the Vault does not send or save anything itself. When you tap “Text it” or “Email it,” the form just opens your own text or email app with your message already filled in. Nothing is transmitted until you press send in your own app — and when you do, the message goes through your own carrier or email provider, the same as any other text or email you send.

Calling, texting, and emailing David

If you choose to call, text, or email, David receives what you send — your number or email address and your message — so he can reply and help with your order. That contact happens through your phone and email services, not through this app, and the app keeps no copy of it.

Links to other sites

Some buttons open other sites and apps — eBay, Whatnot, TikTok Shop, YouTube, and Instagram. Anything you do there, including buying, is covered by that service's own privacy policy, not this one.

Changes & questions

If this policy ever changes, the updated version will be posted here in the app. Questions about privacy? Ask David directly.
